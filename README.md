# Data-Science-practise-PRT661
Long term health condition analysis and risk identifier project 

Project goal:

As part of our Master of Data Science course, we are developing a Long Term Health Condition Analysis and Risk likelyhood identifier based on the following cultural
and diverse factors of Australia, from the 2021 data from Australia Institute of Health and Welfare.

Here we used table 10, 13 and 14 which consisted of few different features which we deemed useful for our analysis and predictions.

The following is a description of the different variables from the dataset: 

Long-term health conditions (LTHCs): measures the number of people who reported that they have been told by a doctor or nurse that they have any long-term health conditions. Long-term health conditions (LTHCs) include health conditions that have lasted or are expected to last six months or more, may occur from time to time, are controlled by medication or are in remission. 

Country of birth of person: Contains records of the country of birth of that individual, according to the Standard Australian Classification of Countries (SACC), 2016. Names, date of births or other identifiable variables have not been included in this dataset. 

Language used at home:  Classified according to the Australian Standard Classification of Languages (ASCL), 2016. 

Years spent in Australia: Specified how long that person has been in Australia. 

Proficiency in spoken English: Categorized into two groups of “very well” and “not well at all”. 

Country of birth. 

Age group: Age groups classified between “0 to 44”, “44 to 64” and “64 to above” 

Sex: Categorized between “Male”, “Female” and “Persons”. 

Number of people reporting with LTHC. 

Population: The total recorded population from the census, including those with and without LTHCs. 

Age specific percentage population of people with LTHC: Percentage calculated by dividing the number of people with LTHC by population
